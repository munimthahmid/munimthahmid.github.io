FORMAL METHODS · TRUSTWORTHY SOFTWARE

Munim Thahmid

Research Intern, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign
Recent CSE graduate, BUET

My research focuses on making software systems more trustworthy as AI-generated code becomes increasingly common. I want to use formal specifications, theorem provers, and model checkers both to verify AI-generated software and to give LLMs machine-checkable feedback while they reason. My long-term goal is to improve their ability to produce correct specifications, proofs, and safe, verifiable code from the beginning.

Research interests: formal verification, LLM reasoning and verification, AI-assisted specification and proof generation, dependable distributed systems, and software reliability.

Research

TLAPS-Bench: Verifier-backed evaluation of frontier LLMs

University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign · Advisor: Prof. Tianyin Xu

I am building TLAPS-Bench, a 956-task benchmark across 71 TLA+ system specifications for evaluating frontier LLMs on proof completion and proof generation. Every answer is replayed and checked by TLAPM, so success means producing a mechanically accepted proof rather than a plausible-looking response.

I evaluate GPT-5.6 Sol, GPT-5.5, Claude Opus 4.8, and Gemini 3.1 Pro across agentic and single-turn settings. On a matched 293-task slice, GPT-5.6 Sol reached 99.0% task pass with iterative verifier feedback versus 18.8% single-turn. My work spans evaluator contracts, canonical replay, anti-cheating checks, reproducible runners, and analysis of how reasoning effort, proof scaffolding, specification complexity, and verifier feedback shape model behavior.

SREGym: AI agents for software reliability

University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign · Current research

I am building SREGym, a live benchmark with 90 realistic SRE problems and 3,623 fault-target pairs. It evaluates whether LLM agents can diagnose and safely recover real cloud and Kubernetes systems under controlled clean and noisy incidents.

My work spans benchmark integration and evaluation infrastructure, including reproducible failure scenarios, state-based recovery oracles, independent agent and judge endpoints, and a nine-question LLM-as-a-Judge rubric validated against experts with Cohen's kappa of 0.90.

Script Matters / BanglishBench

B.Sc. thesis · Multilingual LLM reliability

I built a controlled benchmark with 1,174 aligned Bangla, Banglish, and English items to test whether language-model behavior remains reliable when meaning is held constant but script changes. I evaluated three local Qwen models and five hosted frontier LLMs, including GPT-5.5 and Claude Sonnet 4.6, under frozen prompts and parsers, then traced script-conditioned failures through tokenization, recoverability, and format compliance.

I also fine-tuned Qwen2.5-3B with Hugging Face PEFT/LoRA on Banglish-only and balanced multilingual data, using source-disjoint splits, paired bootstrap intervals, and McNemar tests to measure robustness improvements.

Bengali speech representations

BUET · Supervisor: Dr. Sadia Sharmin

My first-author undergraduate research examined where Bengali phone-like information emerges across Whisper encoder layers using speaker-disjoint probing, cross-model comparison with XLS-R, controlled ablations, and robustness analyses. The resulting paper was accepted to INTERSPEECH 2026.
